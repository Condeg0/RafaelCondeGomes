About Me

Finding Truth in the Noise

My name is Rafael Condé Gomes, and I am a senior at the University of South Florida, pursuing a dual degree in Econometrics & Quantitative Economics and Information Science (Data Science & Analytics). My journey to this field did not begin in a computer lab, but on a tennis court in Brazil. As the #1 ranked junior player in Rio de Janeiro and a top-20 national athlete, I spent years obsessing over performance. In sports, you can lie to yourself about how good you are, but the scoreboard never lies. That early exposure to brutal, objective reality shaped my entire worldview: I trust results, not narratives.

The Crisis of Validity: What the Field Needs Now

The financial and economic sectors are currently facing a crisis of validity. The industry is saturated with methodologies that persist solely due to institutional inertia rather than empirical success. We see this in the dominance of active management, which repeatedly fails to outperform passive benchmarks, and in the reliance on valuation models like Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) or trading multiples. These tools are mathematically elegant, but they often rely on fragile assumptions that crumble under real-world variance.

The critical weakness in the field today is a lack of rigorous performance measurement. Models are often deployed because they are “industry standard,” not because they have been stress-tested against the chaotic reality of actual markets. The industry does not need more analysts who can recite textbook formulas; it needs scientists who can falsify them. My interest in Data Science is driven by this specific gap. I believe the future of finance belongs to those who view markets not through the lens of dogma or “gut feeling,” but through the rigorous application of the scientific method. We must replace “this is how we’ve always done it” with “this is what the evidence proves.”

The Storyteller: Translating Complexity into Action

However, generating evidence is only half the battle. Data is cold, and without context, it is useless to decision-makers. A complex neural network is a black box if a CEO or a client cannot understand its implications for their bottom line. This is where my “soft skills” come into play. I view the Data Scientist not just as a coder, but as a translator.

In my role as Technology Director at Hexa Consulting, I learned that my most valuable skill wasn’t my ability to write Python scripts, but my ability to craft a narrative around the output. I focus on “Data Storytelling”: the art of structuring technical findings into a clear argument that drives action. If I tell a stakeholder that “the model has a 0.78 F1-score,” it means nothing. But if I tell them, “This model identifies the specific inefficiencies that are costing us 15% in wasted capital,” I have solved a problem. My goal is to present complex evidence so clearly that the correct decision becomes obvious to everyone in the room.

Technical Competence & Academic Rigor

My ability to critique current methods is backed by a robust technical foundation. My concentration in Data Science & Analytics has equipped me with a strong stack in Python (Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-Learn, PyTorch) and R for statistical analysis. I don’t just run existing software; I build systems to test my hypotheses.

For instance, in my Credit Risk Optimization project, I didn’t just maximize accuracy; I built a custom engine to maximize profit, proving that I understand the economic reality behind the code. Similarly, my BreakPoint AI project used Hybrid Siamese LSTMs to forecast tennis matches. While standard betting markets rely on static stats, I modeled “momentum” as a time-series problem, achieving parity with institutional bookmakers. These projects are not just academic exercises; they are proof that a data-centric approach can outperform human intuition.
